## [1.6.6] - 2026-08-12 (Library Inventory, manual covers, Companion beta)

EN
### ✨ What's new
* **C66. Library Inventory — what is missing from your library (precursor to #27)** — A new full-width **Inventory** panel answers the question the dashboard never could: which series are incomplete. **Analyze library** runs in the background — it reads your volumes and chapters from Kavita, asks the provider cascade (AniList / MAL / ComicVine, then the backup scrapers) how many there should be, and clusters look-alike series. You get a **Library health** bar (complete / incomplete / expected unknown) with counts and the age of the last analysis, chips for **Missing / Duplicates / No id**, plus **Quick analyze** — which skips the throttled provider calls for series that have not moved since the last pass — and **Cancel**, because a full analysis of a large library takes a while. Each series row carries an `N/M` badge coloured by how complete it is: green when you have everything, cyan when you are up to date on a series still being published, amber then orange then red as it degrades, purple when you own more than the catalogue expects, grey when no catalogue knows the series. A dashed outline marks a count you **forced by hand**, and every forced count is listed in one place with one-click release. **Chapters count as their own unit** (`8/32 ch`) for series filed without volumes. Open a series' report to see stat cards, the missing numbers folded into ranges (`2–13, 18` rather than twelve numbers), the volume-by-volume detail, the forced-expected field, and an **Exclude from inventory** switch for series no catalogue will ever know — they keep being scraped, they just stop skewing the counters. An isolated number far beyond the expected count — an omnibus that Kavita numbered `101` on a 17-issue series — is set aside as **out of range** and named, instead of counting as one more volume and inventing eighty missing numbers. The **Missing details** modal exports to CSV / TXT with the unit stated. Duplicates are scored on both directions of the comparison with edition / spin-off markers, judged at a threshold you set inside the modal, then dismissed or deleted from Kavita. The whole feature switches off from the sidebar (**Library inventory**, `LIBRARY_INVENTORY_ENABLED`): panel, badges and report buttons disappear and the API answers 403, so a tab left open cannot start an analysis. Works across **all libraries** at once. It still only reads — no volume metadata is written and nothing is merged yet. Tests: `tests/test_library_audit.py`, `tests/test_dashboard_renders.py`.
* **C67. Modals: one shell, illustrated, responsive down to a phone** — Every modal now sits on the same shell: one scroll area instead of a scrollbar wherever the layout happened to overflow, a real × button, and actions in a footer that stays put while you scroll a long report. Table-heavy modals (Missing details, Duplicates) widen to 1180 px so the columns stop fighting, and under 720 px the tables fold into labelled cards rather than scrolling sideways — on a phone each modal becomes a full-screen sheet. The cover picker widened to 920 px, because comparing three covers per row is comparing blind. The duplicates modal became a list of aligned rows (title on the left, Kavita and Delete on the right) with the group's score and reasons as chips. Report cards and review-recap tiles line up in equal columns instead of leaving holes. And the modals are finally drawn: every title carries a line-art icon tinted like its header, and empty, loading and error states are an illustration with a title and one line of explanation — a full shelf and a tick for *nothing missing*, a magnifier for *not analysed yet*, an empty crate for *nothing returned*, a warning triangle on failure, a spinning arc while loading (still, if you asked for reduced motion).
* **C68. The statistics card, readable at a glance** — The five figures sat side by side and wrapped after the fourth, leaving *Ignored* alone on a line of its own. They become a legend instead: one line per state, carrying the colour of its segment in the bar, its count and its share of the library, with the states at zero kept on screen but stepped back. The card opens on the number of series tracked, so the percentages have something to hang on. **A sixth state joins them — *Needs seal***, metadata written but Kavita's locks not set, which a file scan can overwrite: the card never showed it although the total counted it, so the figures never quite added up. They do now. Clicking a line filters the series list on that state, and clicking *Ignored* also unhides them, since they are hidden by default. Tests: `tests/test_sidebar_stats_card.py`.
* **C65. Manual covers protected out in the open (🔒 chip + mass escape hatch)** — Protecting a hand-picked cover used to mean silently unticking `cover` in that series' targeted fields: an invisible edit to your own configuration that you could only undo by clicking. Provenance is now tracked on its own, so **your targeted fields are never rewritten behind your back**. The dashboard row shows a clickable **🔒 Manual cover** chip — one click hands the cover back to automatic management — and a new sidebar switch **Overwrite manual covers** (`COVER_FORCE_OVERWRITE`, in the danger block next to Force update) lifts the protection for a whole run: tick it once to rewrite covers after switching provider, instead of clicking series by series. An automatic overwrite drops the mark (the cover belongs to the provider again); an explicit pick, from the Covers modal or a review, always wins and marks it again. Kavita's own cover lock cannot play this role: MetaKavita has to set it on *every* upload, otherwise a Kavita scan regenerates the thumbnail from the files. Tests: `tests/test_cover_manual_protection.py`, `tests/test_routes_series_cover.py`.
* **Magic Input for cover search** — The cover picker now honours a series' Magic URL or ID: the resolved provider is asked for that exact series instead of searching by title. Tests: `tests/test_magic_cover_search.py`.
### 🐛 Bug Fixes
* **BF104. WebLinks replace on force + clear context** — With **Clear Kavita context when forced** + forced update, targeted WebLinks no longer append onto stale Kavita URLs (false-positive leftovers). Links are replaced by this scrape’s set (or cleared if none). Merge/append unchanged when the reset-context option is off. Help text / README updated. Tests: `tests/test_weblinks_reset_context.py`.
* **BF105. Super/Manual Review: hide below-threshold while streaming** — Live candidates under the match threshold no longer flash in before an above-threshold hit arrives. Persistent checkbox **Show below threshold** (`localStorage`); hint when only weak results are hidden; empty-above fallback after scrape completes; orphan master/source selection cleared when weak cards are hidden.
* **BF106. A sync no longer wipes a manually picked cover** — MetaKavita left `coverImageLocked` out of the series update it sends to Kavita, and Kavita read that absence as "the user unlocked the cover": it emptied the cover and scheduled a regeneration from the files. Since a hand-picked cover was also protected from being re-uploaded, the result was a cover destroyed and replaced by a thumbnail cut from the first page. The lock (and the *don't match* flag) are now carried over from Kavita's current state in both update passes. Tests: `tests/test_kavita_api.py`.
* **BF107. Companion Super Review now offers the cover picker** — A Super Review started from the Companion read its options from the dashboard sidebar, which does not exist inside the embedded window: the cover phase was therefore skipped every time, even with **Cover pick** enabled. The embed now receives the server's own settings, and cover previews inside it render without needing a session. Tests: `tests/test_companion_embed_auth.py`.
* **BF108. A silent Kavita library no longer erases your hand-made settings** — Loading the dashboard cleans out series that no longer exist in Kavita. When one library answered with a timeout or a 500, its series were simply absent from the inventory — and treated as deleted: forced id, targeted fields, publisher preference, manual cover mark and any pending review for those series were dropped, with nothing to warn you. The cleanup now requires an inventory read in full, from every library, and says in the live log when it stands down. An empty inventory never purges anything. Tests: `tests/test_orphan_cache_guard.py`.
* **BF109. The Sync button no longer depends on how long the sync takes** — Clicking **Update** on a row ran the whole enrichment inside the HTTP request: a few seconds to several minutes, during which a reverse proxy was free to cut the connection and show you a failure on a series that had in fact been processed. The click now puts the series at the head of the queue, ahead of a running batch, without touching the batch you had queued yourself — including a paused one, whose entries live only in the database. The button is released by an end-of-job signal, so a series parked for manual review reads as a success and an unreachable Kavita releases it at once instead of leaving the row spinning. Two syncs of the same series can no longer overlap either. Tests: `tests/test_force_sync_queued.py`, `tests/test_sync_worker_resilience.py`.
* **BF110. Cover search respects provider rate limits** — Opening the cover picker fires one request per provider, in parallel, and those requests ignored the pacing that enrichment carefully applies: searching for a cover during a batch doubled the traffic sent to each API, which is how a 429 — then an IP ban — arrives. Both paths now share the same pacing per provider. Tests: `tests/test_cover_search_throttle.py`.
* **BF111. A Companion window only ever sees its own series** — The short-lived token the extension uses is issued for one series, from that series' Kavita page, but on the review routes it was accepted for any series: the review queue of the whole library could be read through it, and another series' review confirmed or skipped. Every review route and the live channel now check the series against the token, and mass operations (bulk accept, purge) are refused outright. Tests: `tests/test_companion_embed_auth.py`, `tests/test_socket_handlers.py`.
* **BF112. Signing in during a batch no longer fails** — The accounts table lives in the same file as the cache, but the login path opened it without the settings the rest of the app uses: a login or a password change attempted while a batch or a library analysis was writing could come back as *database is locked*. Both paths now open the database the same way. Tests: `tests/test_auth_db_connection.py`.
* **BF114. One failing series no longer stops every synchronisation** — All syncing goes through a single background worker. An unexpected error outside the enrichment's own safety net — the database momentarily locked, a live-channel emit failing, a missing translation — did not just lose that series: it ended the worker. From then on nothing was processed at all, the batch bar stayed frozen mid-run, and Kavita events and automatic checks piled up in a queue no one was reading, until the container was restarted. Nothing said so. A series that fails is now abandoned on its own, with its reason in the log, the batch bar moves on, and the queue keeps draining. The automatic check survives an unreadable configuration the same way. Tests: `tests/test_sync_worker_resilience.py`.
* **BF113. Labels that stayed in English** — Five strings — the row's **Options** button and its progress label, the cover step's two keyboard hints, the Companion timeout message — were never handed to the interface's dictionary, so they showed in English whatever language you had chosen; the row result labels were written straight into the code. All of them go through the dictionary now. Tests: `tests/test_frontend_i18n_and_escaping.py`.
* **BF121. A manual review no longer lowers the match threshold for the whole app** — Opening a Manual or Super Review drops the provider acceptance threshold to zero on purpose, so you get to judge the weak matches instead of losing them. That drop was applied process-wide: an automatic enrichment running at the same time on *another* series accepted its very first candidate whatever its score, and wrote a stranger's summary, cover and authors into Kavita without a word. Worse, two overlapping collections left the threshold at zero for good, until the container was restarted — from then on every series was enriched with the first thing any provider returned. The drop is now scoped to the collection that asked for it, and nests safely; the parallel scrapers of that collection still inherit it. Tests: `tests/test_match_threshold_portee.py`.
* **BF122. Writing external ids no longer wipes a manually picked cover** — Same cause as BF106, in the call that stores the AniList / MyAnimeList / MangaBaka ids: it left the cover lock out of the payload, and Kavita read that absence as an unlock — it emptied the cover and scheduled a regeneration from the files. That call goes out first, before the cover step, which is precisely the step skipped for a hand-picked cover: the cover was destroyed with nothing put back. The lock and the *don't match* flag are now carried over from Kavita's current state, as they already were in the two other update paths. Tests: `tests/test_external_ids_write.py`.
* **BF123. Weak candidates are no longer announced as strong ones** — Cards streamed into the review modal while the scrapers were still running were all filed above the threshold, badge-free and confirmation-free, even at a score of 0.20 — the band was computed with the lowered collection threshold instead of your real one. It was also stored that way, so a scrape that never finished (crash, restart) left the queue full of false matches that **Accept all** would have applied in bulk. The band now always uses your threshold, and the streamed cards agree with the final result. Tests: `tests/test_match_threshold_portee.py`.
* **BF124. A refused external-id write no longer reads as a success** — Kavita's answer to the AniList / MyAnimeList / MangaBaka id write was never read: the series went green as *completed* with those fields still empty, and since that status is terminal nothing ever tried again. The refusal is now logged and the series is not marked completed — everything that did get written stays written. Re-run the series in forced mode to retry the ids. Tests: `tests/test_external_ids_write.py`.
* **BF125. The end of a scrape no longer undoes what you just did** — Candidates are collected while the review modal is already open and usable, and the end of that collection rebuilt the review from scratch. Skipping a review during the scrape made it vanish and then come back a few seconds later; picking a provider during the scrape sent the review back to the picking step, losing the preview and the choice. The end of a scrape now only refreshes the list of candidates: it never resurrects a review you passed on, and never walks back a choice you made. Tests: `tests/test_manual_review_finalize_etat.py`.
* **BF126. A re-scrape no longer destroys the review you are examining** — A batch launched on an explicit selection, or a plain Kavita event, went over a series already parked for manual review: starting a review replaces the one that series already had, and in manual mode that replacement happens before anything is even scraped. The open modal was left pointing at a review that no longer existed — *review not found* on confirm, and all the work to redo. A series in review is no longer re-scraped by a non-forced pass, and the Kavita summary no longer enters that decision (a series is in review precisely because it has none). Re-running the scrape on purpose — the row button, forced mode, the Companion — still replaces the review, and a replacement now leaves a trace in the log. Tests: `tests/test_pending_review_not_clobbered.py`.
* **BF127. A hiccup during the inventory no longer files a series as “no external id”** — A series whose analysis failed (Kavita briefly unavailable) was written as having no external id and shown with an empty badge, although it did have an AniList id: the filter and the counter contradicted each other. An analysis that reached no conclusion now writes nothing and claims nothing on screen; the previous verdict stands. A cancelled analysis leaves no partial marking either, just like library counters and duplicates. Tests: `tests/test_hygiene_scan_failures.py`.
* **BF128. MangaUpdates: a validated match is no longer lost when the detail call fails** — The search already builds the full series record; a second request fetches the detailed version, and the API sits behind Cloudflare. A 403, a 429 or a dropped connection on that second request threw everything away: the log announced *match validated (87%)* and the provider then contributed nothing. The record from the search is now used as a fallback, the detail still taking precedence when it answers. Tests: `tests/test_scraper_mangaupdates_detail_fallback.py`.
* **BF129. Manual review: the right arrow brings back the series as you left it** — Moving to the next series with the keyboard dropped you back on the candidate list even for a series whose provider you had already picked: everything had to be done again. The right arrow now follows exactly the same path as the left one, which restores the edit panel, the merge sources and the preview — and the previous series' preview is no longer kept in memory. Tests: `tests/test_frontend_review_navigation.py`.
* **BF130. ComicVine: the issue search no longer mixes up two same-named runs** — When no volume comes out of the title, ComicVine is queried for issues, and the first result was taken as is: its parent volume became the series written, with no regard for the run year — while the volume searches do use it. Nothing caught the mistake afterwards, the final check comparing titles that are identical from one run to the next: “Batman (1940)” could be written onto a “Batman (2011)”. Issues are now ranked by title and by their publication date. Tests: `tests/test_scraper_comicvine_issue_pass.py`.
* **BF131. The library health bar now accounts for the series it could not analyze** — When a series' analysis fails mid-run — Kavita unavailable for a moment — it keeps its previous verdict, which is the honest thing to do; but it then belonged to no segment at all: complete + incomplete + expected unknown stopped adding up to the number of series the bar announced, and nothing on screen explained the gap. Those series now get a hatched segment of their own, **Not analyzed**, counted alongside the others. The label only shows up when there is something to report, and says what to do about it: run the analysis again and they will be picked up. Tests: `tests/test_hygiene_health_serie_en_echec.py`.
* **BF132. An Inventory export can no longer run a formula when you open it** — A series whose title starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` was written into the CSV exactly as it stood, and Excel or LibreOffice reads such a cell as a formula to evaluate — including the one that asks the system to open a program. Every text value in the three CSV exports (series report, missing volumes, duplicates) is now flagged as text so the spreadsheet displays it instead of running it; the cell still reads the same. TXT exports are untouched. Tests: `tests/test_export_csv_injection_formule.py`.
* **BF133. AniList no longer gives up on a series with a sparse record** — Providers do not always omit what they do not have: they answer *empty*. AniList does exactly that for the cover, the release year, the staff and the characters, and reading those answers crashed the whole scrape for that series: AniList contributed nothing, with only a laconic *AniList error* in the log to show for it. The same fragility was hiding in Hardcover, Kitsu, MangaDex and Open Library — a search answered without results, a book with no listed author, a cover with no file. All of them now treat *empty* as *absent*. Tests: `tests/test_scrapers_champs_nullables.py`.
* **BF134. Bédéthèque: a maintenance page is no longer taken for a series record** — The two pages Bédéthèque is asked for were parsed without ever looking at what the site answered: a 404, a 503 or the maintenance page was read like a record. Its heading became the fetched title, its *back in a few minutes* message the summary, and the publication status kept its default, *Finished*. Coming from a Bédéthèque URL you had pinned yourself, that page was accepted with a perfect score and could be written into Kavita. Both reads now check the answer, name the real cause in the log — the HTTP code and the address — and fall back on the album page when the series page is the one that failed. Tests: `tests/test_scraper_bedetheque_http.py`.
* **BF135. “Story & Art”: the mangaka is credited as artist too** — Providers describe combined roles in one line, and *Story & Art* — the most common case for a manga — was filed under writers only: half the mangas arrived in Kavita with no penciller at all, and nothing said so. *Cover Illustration* and *Cover Art*, read as plain artwork, landed in the pencillers instead of the cover artists. Composite roles are now split and each part credited where it belongs, the most precise reading winning: a cover stays a cover, a colour credit stays a colour credit. Tests: `tests/test_kavita_payload_roles_staff.py`.
* **BF136. The manual review list no longer stops at 200 without saying so** — The list view only ever asked the server for its first 200 reviews, while the badge counts the whole queue: after a big batch in manual review mode, the two contradicted each other. Worse, once those 200 were dealt with, the modal showed the *all done* recap — with hundreds of series still waiting. The list now says what it is showing (*the 200 oldest of 640*), and finishing a page fetches the next one instead of announcing the end. Loading the entire queue at once was the other option; every review carries its candidate cards, summaries included, and opening the modal on a large library would have paid for it. Tests: `tests/test_frontend_review_file_paginee.py`.
* **BF137. “Accept all ≥ threshold” now really accepts everything above the value you set** — The slider goes down to 30% precisely so you can sweep up the weak matches in one gesture, but only the candidates already above your match threshold were considered: a review whose only candidate scored 50% was counted as *left in queue* although you had asked for 30%, with nothing to explain it. The value you set now applies to every candidate. Two safeguards, so accepting weak matches in bulk does not become a trap: the slider's floor also applies on the server side, and the ones accepted below your match threshold are counted separately in the result (*including 12 weak matches — worth a check*) and traced as weak picks in your stats, exactly as if you had picked them by hand. Tests: `tests/test_manual_review_bulk_accept_bande_basse.py`.
* **BF138. A broken configuration no longer locks a series out of syncing until restart** — A series is flagged as *being processed* for as long as its enrichment runs, so that two paths cannot write to Kavita at the same time. That flag was set a fraction too early: if reading the configuration failed right then — a `config.json` truncated by an interrupted restore, for instance — the flag was never cleared. That series was refused with *Already being processed* by every later sync, from the row button to the automatic check, until the container was restarted, and no message ever tied it back to the configuration. The flag is now released whatever happens. Tests: `tests/test_enrich_series_verrou_libere.py`.
* **Companion 1.0.23 — POSIX zip paths** — Sideload zips packed on Windows no longer store backslash entry names (broken folders on Linux/macOS). Pure Node zip writer in `companion/scripts/pack.mjs`.
* **Companion 1.0.24 — same-host reverse proxy ([#34](https://github.com/raukorim-bot/MetaKavita/issues/34))** — Kavita + Meta on one host (`/kavita` + `/metakavita`) no longer blocked as “This is MetaKavita”. Meta detected via `metaBaseUrl` path prefix (`isMetaKavitaUrl`).
* **Companion 1.0.25 / 1.0.26 — cover previews finally show up, even on an HTTPS Kavita** — MangaDex and Anime-Planet covers travel through MetaKavita, and inside a Kavita page those thumbnails were being requested from Kavita itself — or, on an HTTPS Kavita served by an HTTP MetaKavita, blocked outright by the browser as mixed content. The extension now fetches the image bytes itself, in the background, and hands them to the picker inline; direct HTTPS covers keep loading as before. A preview that comes back as the MetaKavita login page now says so instead of staying blank — the sign that the MetaKavita container still runs an older build.
* **Companion 1.0.26 — Super Review in a window of its own** — An HTTPS Kavita cannot embed a review panel served over HTTP, and no extension trick lifts that. Super Review now opens in a small dedicated window centred over Kavita instead of an embedded panel: it still focuses Kavita and closes itself when the review is done, and falls back to a plain tab if your popup blocker gets in the way.
* **Companion 1.0.27 — the webhook token leaves the reach of the Kavita page** — The extension's settings panel is drawn inside the Kavita page, and it was drawn in a way the page could read back: the webhook token was written into its field on every series page, panel open or not, and one line of script on that page was enough to take it. That token issues access to any series in MetaKavita — reading and confirming its reviews, changing its cover. The panel is now sealed, and the token only exists on screen while you have the panel open. **The cover picker was giving away a key of its own**: the address of each preview carried the short-lived pass that opens that series — reviews included — and an address is something the page can simply read. Previews now travel through the extension, which presents the pass itself, out of sight; and opening the picker asks MetaKavita for **one** pass for the whole grid instead of one per cover. In the same pass, an old iframe interface that nothing had loaded for several versions was deleted: it was still being shipped in both downloads, still accepting instructions from any window on the page — including one telling it to cover Kavita with a page of someone else's choosing — and the extension now exposes a single file to websites instead of its whole toolbox. **Two messages that were showing you their own internal name** are fixed: the one announcing Super Review's dedicated window, and the one explaining a refused cover preview. And **an address like `localhost:5011` is finally accepted** — typed without `http://`, a named host was mistaken for a protocol, and the failure came back as a permission problem, which it never was. Extension checks now run in continuous integration: no release can ship a translation used without being written, a script that does not parse, or a download that has fallen behind its sources. Tests: `tests/test_companion_hardening.py`, `companion/scripts/selfcheck-i18n.mjs`, `verify-dist.mjs`.

### ✨ Nouveautés
* **C66. Inventaire bibliothèque — ce qui manque dans votre bibliothèque (précurseur #27)** — Un nouveau panneau **Inventaire**, pleine largeur, répond à la question que le dashboard ne savait pas traiter : quelles séries sont incomplètes. **Analyser la bibliothèque** travaille en arrière-plan — il lit vos tomes et vos chapitres dans Kavita, demande à la cascade de providers (AniList / MAL / ComicVine, puis les scrapers de secours) combien il devrait y en avoir, et regroupe les séries qui se ressemblent. Vous obtenez une barre **Santé de la bibliothèque** (complètes / incomplètes / attendu inconnu) avec les compteurs et l'âge de la dernière analyse, des chips **Manquants / Doublons / Sans id**, plus une **Analyse rapide** — qui épargne les appels providers throttlés aux séries qui n'ont pas bougé depuis le dernier passage — et **Annuler**, parce qu'une analyse complète d'une grosse bibliothèque prend du temps. Chaque ligne de série porte une cartouche `N/M` colorée selon sa complétion : vert quand tout y est, cyan quand vous êtes à jour sur une série encore en cours de parution, ambre puis orange puis rouge à mesure que ça se dégrade, violet quand vous en avez plus que le catalogue n'en annonce, gris quand aucun catalogue ne connaît la série. Un liseré pointillé signale un compte que vous avez **forcé à la main**, et tous les forçages sont listés au même endroit, avec libération en un clic. **Les chapitres comptent comme leur propre unité** (`8/32 ch`) pour les séries rangées sans tomes. Ouvrez le rapport d'une série pour voir des cartes de synthèse, les numéros manquants pliés en intervalles (`2–13, 18` plutôt que douze numéros), le détail tome par tome, le champ d'attendu forcé et une case **Exclure de l'inventaire** pour les séries qu'aucun catalogue ne connaîtra jamais — elles restent scrapées, elles cessent simplement de fausser les compteurs. Un numéro isolé très au-delà de l'attendu — une intégrale que Kavita a numérotée `101` sur une série de 17 numéros — est mis de côté comme **hors plage** et nommé, au lieu de compter pour un tome de plus et d'inventer quatre-vingts manquants. La modale **Détail manquants** s'exporte en CSV / TXT, unité indiquée. Les doublons sont notés dans les deux sens de la comparaison avec des marqueurs d'édition et de spin-off, jugés au seuil que vous réglez dans la modale, puis ignorés ou supprimés de Kavita. Toute la fonctionnalité s'éteint depuis la sidebar (**Inventaire de la bibliothèque**, `LIBRARY_INVENTORY_ENABLED`) : panneau, cartouches et boutons de rapport disparaissent et l'API répond 403, pour qu'un onglet resté ouvert ne lance pas d'analyse. Fonctionne sur **toutes les bibliothèques** à la fois. Il ne fait toujours que lire : aucune métadonnée de volume n'est écrite et rien n'est encore fusionné. Tests : `tests/test_library_audit.py`, `tests/test_dashboard_renders.py`.
* **C67. Modales : un seul socle, illustrées, responsive jusqu'au téléphone** — Toutes les modales reposent désormais sur le même socle : une seule zone de défilement au lieu d'une barre de scroll partout où la mise en page débordait, un vrai bouton ×, et des actions dans un pied qui ne bouge plus quand vous parcourez un long rapport. Les modales à tableau (Détail manquants, Doublons) s'élargissent à 1180 px pour que les colonnes cessent de se disputer la place, et sous 720 px les tableaux se replient en cartes étiquetées au lieu de défiler sur le côté — sur téléphone, chaque modale devient une feuille plein écran. Le cover picker passe à 920 px, parce que comparer trois couvertures par ligne revient à comparer à l'aveugle. La modale doublons devient une liste de lignes alignées (titre à gauche, Kavita et Supprimer à droite) avec le score et les raisons du groupe en pastilles. Les cartes du rapport et les tuiles du récap de review s'alignent en colonnes égales au lieu de laisser des trous. Et les modales sont enfin dessinées : chaque titre porte un pictogramme au trait teinté comme son en-tête, et les états vides, de chargement et d'erreur deviennent une illustration avec un titre et une ligne d'explication — étagère pleine et coche pour *aucun manquant*, loupe pour *pas encore analysé*, caisse vide pour *rien à afficher*, triangle d'alerte en cas d'échec, arc tournant pendant le chargement (immobile si vous avez demandé moins d'animations).

Au passage, une ancienne interface en iframe que plus rien ne chargeait depuis plusieurs versions a été supprimée : elle continuait d'être livrée dans les deux téléchargements, d'accepter des instructions de n'importe quelle fenêtre de la page — y compris celle de recouvrir Kavita d'une page choisie par un tiers — et l'extension n'expose plus qu'un seul fichier aux sites web au lieu de toute sa boîte à outils. **Deux messages qui vous montraient leur nom interne** sont corrigés : celui qui annonce la fenêtre dédiée de la Super Review, et celui qui explique un aperçu de couverture refusé. Les vérifications de l'extension tournent maintenant en intégration continue : plus aucune version ne peut partir avec une traduction utilisée sans être écrite, un script qui ne se lit pas, ou un téléchargement en retard sur ses sources. ### 🔒 Security
* **BF46. Dependency CVE bumps (thanks angusmaul)** — `gunicorn` `21.2.0` → `23.0.0` and `requests` `2.31.0` → `2.33.1`. Clears five known advisories: two request-smuggling issues in Gunicorn (`PYSEC-2026-1433`, `PYSEC-2026-1434`, both fixed in 22.0.0) and three in Requests (`PYSEC-2026-1873`, `PYSEC-2026-1872`, `PYSEC-2026-2275`, the last only fixed in 2.33.0). No public API change in either package for the way MetaKavita uses them. `googletrans` is deliberately left at `4.0.0-rc1`.
* **BF47. `/api/proxy-image` size cap (thanks angusmaul)** — The proxy now streams the remote response and refuses anything over **5 MB** with a `413`, instead of buffering the whole body into memory with `res.content`. An allowlisted host serving a very large file could previously exhaust the container's memory, which under `gunicorn -w 1` takes down the whole application. `Content-Length` is checked first as a cheap early reject; the running byte total is what actually enforces the limit, since that header can be absent or untrue. Redirect hops are now closed as they are followed (`url_allowlist.fetch_with_safe_redirects`), which matters once responses are streamed.
* **BF48. Webhook token as a header (thanks angusmaul)** — `/webhook` accepts `X-Webhook-Token` in addition to the existing `?token=` query parameter, which keeps working unchanged. The header is preferred because a query string ends up in reverse-proxy access logs, browser history and `Referer` headers. Token comparison now runs on UTF-8 bytes, so a non-ASCII token returns a clean `401` instead of raising inside `secrets.compare_digest`.
* **BF49. `config.json` written 0600 (thanks angusmaul)** — `save_config()` restricts the file to its owner after every write. It holds `SECRET_KEY`, `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` and every API key, and was previously created with the process umask (0644 — world-readable — on a default Docker image). Applied on every save so a file restored from a backup or written by an older version is repaired too. Best-effort: `chmod` is skipped silently on Windows and on filesystems that refuse it, and can never fail a save.
* **C54. Non-root container with PUID/PGID (thanks angusmaul)** — The image no longer runs as root. It ships a dedicated `metakavita` user defaulting to **1000:1000**, and a linuxserver.io-style entrypoint applies `PUID`/`PGID` at *runtime* before dropping privileges with `gosu`, so a bind-mounted `./data` owned by any uid keeps working. Gunicorn still runs as PID 1, so `docker stop` reaches it directly and shuts down cleanly. **Upgrading:** an existing `data/` is root-owned from previous versions — the entrypoint takes ownership of it automatically on every start, including sideloaded scrapers under `data/scrapers/`. A `HEALTHCHECK` is included; with **C57** it probes `GET /healthz` and requires HTTP **200** (no longer `/login` with a lax status check).
* **C55. `.dockerignore` added (thanks angusmaul)** — `COPY . .` previously copied whatever was in the build context. On any machine where the application had been run, that included `data/config.json` with a live `SECRET_KEY`, `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` and API keys, baked into an image layer. Published `ghcr.io` images were never affected (CI builds from a clean checkout), but local builds were. Also drops local virtualenvs, `.git/` and the test suite from the image — 268 MB → 185 MB.
* **C56. Custom scraper RCE warning (thanks angusmaul)** — `CUSTOM_SCRAPERS.md` now opens with a prominent warning, in French and English, that a `.py` dropped into `data/scrapers/` is imported and executed at startup with the application's full privileges: no sandbox, no validation. Spells out what a malicious scraper can actually reach (`config.json` and therefore `SECRET_KEY` / `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` / every API key, any file the container can see, arbitrary outbound connections, and widening the image-proxy allowlist via `proxy_domains`), gives non-programmers concrete red flags to look for, calls out AI-generated code specifically, and states plainly that no third-party scraper is vetted by anyone until the community repository exists. A short version with a link is added to both README security sections.

### 🔐 Authentication
* **C58. Full user/password authentication (issue #15)** — Replaces the single optional plaintext `ADMIN_PASSWORD` with a real account system. A `users` table in `cache.db` stores a username and a Werkzeug hash (`pbkdf2:sha256`, method pinned explicitly rather than following Werkzeug's shifting default). On first run every route redirects to a `/setup` screen that creates the account; `/setup` closes permanently once an account exists, so it cannot become an unauthenticated way to add a second one.
* **Fails closed.** The old gate only protected the application *if* `ADMIN_PASSWORD` happened to be set, which meant the default install served the dashboard to anyone. There is now no configuration in which the UI is reachable without a session — including when the database is unreadable, where access is denied rather than falling through to "no account, let them in". The Socket.IO handshake is gated too: it bypasses Flask's `before_request` stack, so without that the live log, batch progress and cover streams would have stayed readable without an account.
* **Forced migration, no silent import.** An existing plaintext `ADMIN_PASSWORD` is never adopted as the new password — it lived in cleartext on disk, so hashing it would only protect an already-compromised secret. It is deleted from `config.json` once the real account is created.
* **Password change** — Config modal "Account" section (`POST /account/password`) to change the password without leaving the app. Re-verifies the current password through the same check as `/login`; a wrong one counts as a failed login attempt under the existing brute-force lockout.
* **`TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT`** — `1` by default (unchanged behaviour behind a reverse proxy); `0` makes MetaKavita ignore `X-Forwarded-*` and count the real TCP peer. This matters: `ProxyFix` takes `remote_addr` from `X-Forwarded-For`, so on a directly-exposed instance an attacker could rotate that header and the per-IP lockout would never trigger.
* **Brute-force lockout** — 5 failed attempts from one IP locks that IP for 15 minutes, checked before any password hashing so attempts stay cheap. Temporary by design, so a typo cannot permanently lock anyone out. Held in memory, which is sound because Gunicorn runs `-w 1`; writing to SQLite on every failed attempt would hand an unauthenticated attacker unlimited disk writes.
* **Global lockout backstop** — 20 failed logins in 15 minutes across *all* addresses also locks the login screen. Closes the hole where `TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT=1` on a directly-exposed instance let an attacker rotate `X-Forwarded-For` and never trip the per-IP counter. When it fires it locks everyone for up to 15 minutes — intentional; the per-IP lockout is what stops an attacker without delaying you.
* **Legacy ownership proof on `/setup`** — if `ADMIN_PASSWORD` is still in `config.json`, setup asks for it once before creating the account, then deletes it. Closes the upgrade claim window where an empty `users` table would otherwise let the first network visitor own a previously password-protected instance. Fresh installs (no legacy password) stay first-come-first-served; pre-provision with `ADMIN_PASSWORD_HASH` if that matters.
* **`ADMIN_PASSWORD_HASH` / `ADMIN_USERNAME`** — optional pre-hashed seeding for docker-compose deployments, generated by the new `debug/hash_password.py`. A separate variable rather than overloading `ADMIN_PASSWORD`, since telling a hash from a plaintext by inspection is exactly the ambiguity that produces auth bugs. Ignored once an account exists, so a forgotten variable can never overwrite a real password. A value that is not a Werkzeug hash shape is refused with a log error — never written — so a plaintext pasted by mistake cannot create an account no password can open.
* **Timing equalization** — unknown usernames and wrong passwords both pay exactly one memoized KDF against a dummy hash (not a fresh `generate_password_hash` per miss, which previously cost *two* KDFs and inverted the timing signal).
* **Socket.IO handshake** — unauthenticated connects are rejected with `return False` (documented Flask-SocketIO form). Per-event `_reject_unauthenticated` guard as defense in depth; queue/cover emits target the connecting `sid` only.
* **Sessions** — keyed on `user_id`; `PERMANENT_SESSION_LIFETIME` set explicitly to 7 days (Flask's silent default was 31).

### 🩺 Operations
* **C57. `GET /healthz` liveness endpoint** — Returns `{"status": "ok", "version": …}` and nothing else. Whitelisted in `require_login`, so it keeps answering `200` once a password is set, which is what makes it usable as the container's `HEALTHCHECK` target — that check previously had to hit `/login` and accept any status below 500, because no route reliably returned `200`. It now demands exactly `200`, so a routing regression is caught instead of passing as a `404`. The endpoint reads no configuration, opens no database connection and never contacts Kavita: it reports that the application is alive and routing, not that its dependencies are up, so a Kavita outage cannot put a healthy container into a restart loop.
* **Compose / environment seeding** — On a fresh install, `load_config()` merges environment variables before writing defaults. Precedence: `config.json` > environment > default. `ADMIN_PASSWORD` is intentionally *not* seeded from the environment (would re-arm the one-shot ownership proof forever).

### 🔒 Sécurité
* **BF46. Montée de versions (CVE, merci angusmaul)** — `gunicorn` `21.2.0` → `23.0.0` et `requests` `2.31.0` → `2.33.1`. Corrige cinq vulnérabilités connues : deux failles de *request smuggling* dans Gunicorn (`PYSEC-2026-1433`, `PYSEC-2026-1434`, corrigées en 22.0.0) et trois dans Requests (`PYSEC-2026-1873`, `PYSEC-2026-1872`, `PYSEC-2026-2275`, cette dernière uniquement corrigée en 2.33.0). Aucun changement d'API publique pour l'usage qu'en fait MetaKavita. `googletrans` reste volontairement en `4.0.0-rc1`.
* **BF47. Plafond de taille sur `/api/proxy-image` (merci angusmaul)** — Le proxy lit désormais la réponse distante en flux et refuse au-delà de **5 Mo** avec un `413`, au lieu de charger tout le corps en mémoire via `res.content`. Un hôte autorisé servant un très gros fichier pouvait épuiser la mémoire du conteneur — ce qui, sous `gunicorn -w 1`, emporte toute l'application. Le `Content-Length` sert de refus précoce peu coûteux ; c'est le total courant des octets lus qui applique réellement la limite, cet en-tête pouvant être absent ou mensonger. Les hops de redirection sont maintenant fermés au fil de leur suivi (`url_allowlist.fetch_with_safe_redirects`), ce qui compte dès lors que les réponses sont streamées.
* **BF48. Jeton du webhook en en-tête (merci angusmaul)** — `/webhook` accepte `X-Webhook-Token` en plus du paramètre `?token=` existant, qui continue de fonctionner à l'identique. L'en-tête est recommandé car une chaîne de requête se retrouve dans les logs d'accès des reverse proxies, l'historique du navigateur et les en-têtes `Referer`. La comparaison se fait désormais sur les octets UTF-8 : un jeton non-ASCII renvoie un `401` propre au lieu de faire lever `secrets.compare_digest`.
* **BF49. `config.json` écrit en 0600 (merci angusmaul)** — `save_config()` restreint le fichier à son propriétaire après chaque écriture. Il contient `SECRET_KEY`, `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` et toutes les clés d'API, et était créé avec l'umask du processus (0644 — lisible par tous — sur une image Docker par défaut). Réappliqué à chaque sauvegarde, afin de corriger aussi un fichier restauré d'une sauvegarde ou écrit par une version antérieure. Best-effort : le `chmod` est ignoré silencieusement sous Windows et sur les systèmes de fichiers qui le refusent, et ne peut jamais faire échouer une sauvegarde.
* **C54. Conteneur non-root avec PUID/PGID (merci angusmaul)** — L'image ne tourne plus en root. Elle embarque un utilisateur dédié `metakavita` par défaut en **1000:1000**, et un entrypoint façon linuxserver.io applique `PUID`/`PGID` au *démarrage* avant d'abandonner les privilèges via `gosu` : un `./data` monté et possédé par n'importe quel uid continue donc de fonctionner. Gunicorn reste PID 1, donc `docker stop` l'atteint directement et l'arrêt est propre. **Mise à jour :** un `data/` existant appartient à root depuis les versions précédentes — l'entrypoint en reprend la propriété automatiquement à chaque démarrage, y compris les scrapers sideloadés dans `data/scrapers/`. Un `HEALTHCHECK` est fourni ; avec **C57** il interroge `GET /healthz` et exige HTTP **200** (plus `/login` avec un contrôle de statut laxiste).
* **C55. Ajout d'un `.dockerignore` (merci angusmaul)** — `COPY . .` copiait auparavant tout le contenu du contexte de build. Sur une machine où l'application avait déjà tourné, cela incluait `data/config.json` avec un `SECRET_KEY`, un `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` et des clés d'API réels, figés dans une couche d'image. Les images publiées sur `ghcr.io` n'ont jamais été concernées (la CI build depuis un checkout propre), mais les builds locaux l'étaient. Exclut aussi les virtualenvs locaux, `.git/` et la suite de tests — 268 Mo → 185 Mo.
* **C56. Avertissement RCE sur les scrapers personnalisés (merci angusmaul)** — `CUSTOM_SCRAPERS.md` s'ouvre désormais sur un avertissement bien visible, en français et en anglais : un `.py` déposé dans `data/scrapers/` est importé et exécuté au démarrage avec tous les droits de l'application, sans bac à sable ni validation. Détaille ce qu'un scraper malveillant peut réellement atteindre (`config.json` et donc `SECRET_KEY` / `WEBHOOK_TOKEN` / toutes les clés d'API, n'importe quel fichier visible du conteneur, des connexions sortantes arbitraires, et l'élargissement de l'allowlist du proxy d'images via `proxy_domains`), donne des signaux d'alarme concrets pour les non-développeurs, vise explicitement le code généré par IA, et précise sans détour qu'aucun scraper tiers n'est vérifié tant que le dépôt communautaire n'existe pas. Une version courte avec lien est ajoutée aux deux sections sécurité du README.

### 🔐 Authentification
* **C58. Authentification utilisateur/mot de passe complète (issue #15)** — Remplace l'unique `ADMIN_PASSWORD` optionnel en clair par un vrai système de comptes. Une table `users` dans `cache.db` stocke un identifiant et un hachage Werkzeug (`pbkdf2:sha256`, méthode épinglée explicitement plutôt que de suivre le défaut mouvant de Werkzeug). Au premier démarrage, toutes les routes redirigent vers un écran `/setup` qui crée le compte ; `/setup` se ferme définitivement dès qu'un compte existe, pour ne pas devenir un moyen non authentifié d'en créer un second.
* **Fail-closed.** L'ancien gate ne protégeait l'application *que si* `ADMIN_PASSWORD` était renseigné : l'installation par défaut servait donc l'interface à tout le monde. Il n'existe plus aucune configuration dans laquelle l'UI est joignable sans session — y compris base illisible, où l'accès est refusé au lieu de retomber sur « aucun compte, laisse passer ». Le handshake Socket.IO est protégé aussi : il contourne la pile `before_request` de Flask, donc sans cela les flux de logs, de progression des batchs et de couvertures seraient restés lisibles sans compte.
* **Migration forcée, aucune reprise silencieuse.** Un `ADMIN_PASSWORD` en clair existant n'est jamais adopté comme nouveau mot de passe — il a vécu en clair sur le disque, le hacher ne protégerait qu'un secret déjà compromis. Il est supprimé de `config.json` dès que le vrai compte est créé.
* **Changement de mot de passe** — Section « Compte » de la modal Config (`POST /account/password`) pour changer de mot de passe sans quitter l'application. Revérifie le mot de passe actuel par le même contrôle que `/login` ; un mauvais mot de passe compte comme un échec de connexion sous le verrouillage anti-brute-force existant.
* **`TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT`** — `1` par défaut (comportement inchangé derrière un reverse proxy) ; `0` fait ignorer les en-têtes `X-Forwarded-*` et impute la tentative au vrai pair TCP. C'est important : `ProxyFix` tire `remote_addr` de `X-Forwarded-For`, donc sur une instance exposée directement un attaquant pourrait faire varier cet en-tête et le verrouillage par IP ne se déclencherait jamais.
* **Verrouillage anti-force-brute** — 5 échecs depuis une IP la verrouillent 15 minutes, vérifié AVANT tout hachage pour que les tentatives restent peu coûteuses. Temporaire par conception, pour qu'une faute de frappe ne bannisse personne définitivement. Gardé en mémoire, ce qui est sain car Gunicorn tourne en `-w 1` ; écrire en SQLite à chaque échec offrirait à un attaquant non authentifié des écritures disque illimitées.
* **Plafond global** — 20 échecs de connexion en 15 minutes, toutes adresses confondues, verrouillent aussi l'écran de login. Ferme le trou où `TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT=1` sur une instance exposée directement permettait de faire tourner `X-Forwarded-For` sans jamais atteindre le compteur par IP. Quand il se déclenche, tout le monde est bloqué jusqu'à 15 minutes — voulu ; c'est le verrouillage par IP qui arrête un attaquant sans vous retarder.
* **Preuve de propriété legacy sur `/setup`** — si `ADMIN_PASSWORD` est encore dans `config.json`, le setup le demande une fois avant de créer le compte, puis l'efface. Ferme la fenêtre de revendication à la mise à niveau, où une table `users` vide aurait laissé le premier visiteur du réseau s'approprier une instance auparavant protégée. Une installation neuve (pas d'ancien mot de passe) reste au premier arrivé ; pré-provisionnez avec `ADMIN_PASSWORD_HASH` si ça compte.
* **`ADMIN_PASSWORD_HASH` / `ADMIN_USERNAME`** — amorçage pré-haché optionnel pour les déploiements docker-compose, généré par le nouveau `debug/hash_password.py`. Variable distincte plutôt que de surcharger `ADMIN_PASSWORD` : distinguer un hachage d'un texte en clair à l'inspection est exactement l'ambiguïté qui produit des failles d'authentification. Ignoré dès qu'un compte existe, une variable oubliée ne peut donc jamais écraser un mot de passe réel. Une valeur qui n'a pas la forme d'un hachage Werkzeug est refusée avec une erreur de journal — jamais écrite — pour qu'un mot de passe en clair collé par erreur ne crée pas un compte qu'aucun mot de passe n'ouvre.
* **Égalisation de timing** — nom inconnu et mauvais mot de passe paient exactement un KDF mémoïsé contre un hachage factice (plus de `generate_password_hash` frais à chaque miss, qui coûtait *deux* KDF et inversait le signal temporel).
* **Handshake Socket.IO** — connexions non authentifiées refusées par `return False` (forme documentée Flask-SocketIO). Garde `_reject_unauthenticated` par événement en défense en profondeur ; les emits file/couvertures ciblent uniquement le `sid` connecté.
* **Sessions** — indexées sur `user_id` ; `PERMANENT_SESSION_LIFETIME` fixé explicitement à 7 jours (le défaut silencieux de Flask était 31).
